Scientific discovery is defined by the ability to identify the boundaries of existing knowledge and
venture into unexplored territory. The ultimate vision for AI in science is problem-driven autonomous
research: given a fundamental challenge by a human expert, the AI independently navigates the scientific
landscape, uncovers theoretical and empirical bottlenecks, and systematically expands the frontier of
knowledge. In this paper, we introduce ScientistTwo, a fully autonomous multi-agent framework designed
to realize this vision. Specifically, ScientistTwo takes an initial problem as input, establishes state-ofthe-art baselines, formulates novel hypotheses, and coordinates specialized agents to orchestrate an
end-to-end discovery cycle without human intervention. Moreover, the framework rigorously conducts
experiments using diverse datasets and metrics, refines methodologies through automated ablation
studies, and validates research findings via a closed-loop simulated peer-review rebuttal engine. To
evaluate ScientistTwo’s capabilities against the highest standards of human scientific achievement, we
benchmark it across papers accepted at top-tier conferences such as ICLR, ICML, and NeurIPS. As a result,
ScientistTwo autonomously generates expert-level, publishable papers and fully verified, executable
codebases. Its solutions consistently outperform human state-of-the-art models, and achieve higher
average review ratings than human-authored papers under automated AI review agents. These results
show that ScientistTwo is not merely an assistive tool but an autonomous scientific pioneer capable of
pushing the frontiers of human discovery. Project website: https://scientist-two.github.io/

1. Introduction
Scientific discovery has long been the hallmark of human ingenuity, defined by the ability to identify
the boundaries of current knowledge and venture into the unknown. With the rapid advancement of
foundation models (Liu et al., 2024; Singh et al., 2025; Team et al., 2023), artificial intelligence (AI)
is transitioning from a passive conversational assistant to an active participant in the scientific process
(Jansen et al., 2025; Meng et al., 2026; Xu and Peng, 2025). The ultimate ambition of this paradigm
is purely problem-driven autonomous discovery: a human researcher simply specifies a scientific
challenge, and an autonomous AI independently navigates the landscape of human knowledge,
diagnoses theoretical and empirical bottlenecks, formulates novel hypotheses, and executes the
end-to-end research lifecycle on its own to pioneer the human knowledge frontier.
Despite recent progress in the field of autonomous research agents (Tang et al., 2026a; Weng et al.,
2025; Yamada et al., 2025), a substantial gap remains between automated assistant systems and
rigorous empirical scientific standards. Existing systems primarily focus on optimizing single-scalar
metrics on isolated benchmarks, lacking the multi-dimensional reasoning capabilities required to
tackle complex scientific problems (Chen et al., 2026c; Jin et al., 2026). More importantly, current
agents lack the closed-loop empirical rigor of human scientists who continuously iterate based on
evidence. They cannot systematically conduct ablation studies to isolate causal mechanisms for
improvement, nor can they engage in dynamic peer-review processes essential for validating ideas
and addressing methodological critiques through targeted supplementary experiments.

A BSTRACT
Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) subword tokenization is a foundational computational
primitive in large language model (LLM) pipelines, yet its dynamic greedy priority heap maintenance and pointer-chasing search tree raversals present significant throughput bottlenecks in streaming inference and massive data preprocessing. While theoretical incremental algorithms achieve O(log2 t) worst-case time
bounds per byte using Centroid Search Trees (CST), they incur severe instruction overheads, non-contiguous pointer dereferences, and branch mispredictions
on physical CPUs. In this work, we present Vectorized Direct-Interval Suffix Transducer (VD-STrans), an ultra-low-latency incremental BPE framework.
VD-STrans exploits a structural insight into real-world LLM token topologies:
over 99.1% to 99.8% of all Suffix-Successor Trees SufSucTree(ω ) in production
vocabularies contain at most 8 nodes. VD-STrans flattens these compact tree
topologies into 256-bit aligned Compact Flat Decision Vectors (FDVs) and resolves candidate last-token selection via a branchless 256-bit AVX2 SIMD parallel
range comparison kernel in O(1) cycle time. Furthermore, an Atomic Fast-Path
Bypass overlays a 256-bit transition mask on the Aho–Corasick root state to immediately emit single-byte atomic tokens without accessing history state arrays,
while deep trees (> 8 nodes) seamlessly fall back to CST traversals to preserve the
theoretical O(log2 t) worst-case guarantee. Extensive evaluations across 12 major
open-source and proprietary tokenizers (including Llama-4, Qwen-3, DeepSeek3.2, and tiktoken variants) on English, Chinese, and Code text streams demonstrate that VD-STrans achieves a geometric mean throughput speedup of 1.123→
over state-of-the-art incremental BPE (with peak speedups of 1.271→) and up to
23.64→ over standard heap-based BPE, while maintaining 100% exact tokenization fidelity.

To enable streaming processing without full-sequence buffering or regex dependencies, recent work
by Jiang & Gong (2026) introduced an incremental BPE algorithm. By formalizing the Prefix Consistency property (Berglund & van der Merwe, 2023) and introducing Suffix-Successor Trees and
Centroid Search Trees (CST), this approach incrementally updates the valid tokenization prefix in
O(log2 t) worst-case time per byte, supported by fast Aho–Corasick state tracking (Kanda et al.,
2022).
However, a critical performance gap exists between this theoretical tree-search bound and physical
processor execution. Our structural analysis across modern open-source LLM vocabularies reveals
an extreme topology distribution: over 98.6% to 99.8% of all Suffix-Successor Trees SufSucTree(ω )
contain at most 8 nodes, with an average tree depth of only 2 to 3 nodes. Despite this compactness,
existing CST-based incremental algorithms perform pointer-chasing tree traversals, indirect memory
accesses, and branchy binary search iterations during every last-token search step. On modern CPU
architectures, these search loops induce frequent branch mispredictions, cache misses, and pipeline
stalls, preventing incremental tokenizers from reaching peak memory throughput.
To resolve these computational inefficiencies, we propose the Vectorized Direct-Interval Suffix
Transducer (VD-STrans), an ultra-low-latency incremental BPE framework that replaces explicit
search tree traversals with branchless SIMD parallel range evaluation and atomic fast-path bypassing. VD-STrans introduces three core technical innovations:
1. Compact Flat Decision Vectors (FDV): For every canonical token whose SuffixSuccessor Tree contains 8 or fewer nodes (→ 8), VD-STrans flattens the hierarchical
tree topology into a contiguous memory layout storing candidate token IDs, suffix history
offsets |suc(ti )|, and Depth-First Search (DFS) pre-order entry/exit interval timestamps
[Lti , Rti ).
2. Branchless SIMD Parallel Interval Range Kernel: Rather than executing iterative tree
searches or binary loops, VD-STrans fetches history timestamps dfsin(H(|sc| ↑ |suc(ti )|))
for all candidates simultaneously and evaluates interval membership [Lti , Rti ) in parallel using a single 256-bit AVX2 SIMD vector comparison ( mm256 cmpgt epi32 and
mm256 and si256). A bitmask extraction ( mm256 movemask ps) and trailing-zerocount instruction (tzcnt) identify the exact valid last token ε(sc) in O(1) branchless
execution time.
3. Atomic Fast-Path Bypass & Hybrid CST Fallback: VD-STrans incorporates a 256bit transition bitmask on the Aho–Corasick root node to immediately output single-byte
atomic tokens when no multi-byte suffix extensions exist, bypassing history state array
maintenance entirely. For rare deep trees (> 8 nodes, representing < 1% of vocabulary entries), VD-STrans seamlessly falls back to CST search, strictly maintaining the theoretical
O(log2 t) worst-case time bound.

I NTRODUCTION

Subword tokenization is an essential preprocessing step in modern large language model (LLM)
pipelines, serving as the bridge between raw character streams and discrete token representations
(Sennrich et al., 2015; Dubey et al., 2024). As neural architectures and attention computations
have been dramatically accelerated through IO-aware implementations such as FlashAttention (Dao
et al., 2022), text preprocessing operations—traditionally regarded as minor administrative overheads—increasingly emerge as significant latency bottlenecks in real-time inference, streaming text
generation, and massive data ingestion pipelines.
Among various subword segmentation schemes, Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) (Sennrich et al., 2015)
remains the de facto standard due to its deterministic greedy merge logic and effective vocabulary
compression, alongside probabilistic alternatives such as Unigram language modeling (Kudo, 2018;
Bostrom & Durrett, 2020) and regularized variants like BPE-dropout (Provilkov et al., 2019). Standard offline BPE tokenizers (e.g., SentencePiece (Kudo & Richardson, 2018) and tiktoken) employ
dynamic priority heaps to iteratively select highest-priority token pairs. On unpartitioned or long text
sequences, this greedy heap mechanism exhibits O(n log n) to O(n2 ) time complexity, often relying on coarse regex pre-tokenization heuristics to restrict merge scopes at the expense of generality.

ATOMIC FAST-PATH B YPASS AND H YBRID CST FALLBACK

Atomic Fast-Path Bypass. When processing raw text streams, character transitions frequently
land on single-byte atomic tokens that possess no multi-byte suffix extensions in V . To eliminate
unnecessary history array overheads in these cases, VD-STrans overlays a 256-bit transition bitmask
on the root state of the Aho–Corasick automaton.
If the incoming byte transition c matches a root state with no suffix extensions, the atomic bypass
flag triggers immediately. In this fast-path regime, VD-STrans directly emits single-byte token c and
skips history state array indexing, timestamp extraction, and SIMD vector operations entirely.
Hybrid CST Fallback. For rare deep Suffix-Successor Trees where |SufSucTree(ε )| > 8 (representing < 0.8% of vocabulary entries), flat SIMD register capacity is exceeded. VD-STrans maintains theoretical robustness by seamlessly falling back to Centroid Search Tree (CST) decomposition
(Jiang & Gong, 2026). This hybrid control flow preserves the optimal O(log2 t) worst-case per-byte
time complexity, ensuring asymptotic stability on pathological or adversarial inputs.

Incremental tokenization and search-tree structures. Incremental tokenization targets streaming input arriving byte-by-byte. Its theoretical basis is the Prefix Consistency property (Berglund
& van der Merwe, 2023): truncating a valid BPE token sequence yields a valid tokenization of the
corresponding prefix. Building on this, Berglund et al. (2024) study DFA constructions for BPE
transduction and Mamouras et al. (2026) give bounded-delay sequence transduction for linear-time
streaming. To maintain exact standard BPE semantics without delay bounds or regex partitioning, Jiang & Gong (2026) introduced an incremental BPE algorithm over Aho–Corasick automata
(Kanda et al., 2022) and Suffix-Successor Trees, applying Centroid Decomposition (Gagie et al.,
2013) to precompute Centroid Search Trees (CST) with an O(log2 t) per-byte bound. Yet CST
traversals incur heavy pointer-chasing on modern CPUs. VD-STrans resolves this by observing that
> 99% of Suffix-Successor Trees are small (→ 8 nodes), replacing pointer searches with branchless
vector comparisons over flat arrays.
Hardware-accelerated string processing and SIMD. SIMD has driven major throughput gains
in text parsing and state-machine execution. In document parsing, simdjson (Langdale & Lemire,
2019) and Mison (Li et al., 2017) replace FSM control flow with SIMD structural indexing and bitmask operations, eliminating branch mispredictions at multi-GB/s. For comparison-based search,
Khuong & Morin (2015) show that contiguous, cache-aligned layouts with branchless vector instructions outperform pointer-based trees for small sets (N → 16). VD-STrans adapts these principles
to tokenization, mapping DFS interval tests into 256-bit AVX2 register comparisons and converting
branch-heavy tree searches into O(1) branchless data-parallel operations.

Suffix-Successor Tree. For any canonical token ε ↑ V , the Suffix-Successor Tree, denoted as
SufSucTree(ε ), is the subgraph of Fsuc induced by all canonical tokens that form suffixes of ε .
Because all candidate suffix tokens of sc share the same final character c, SufSucTree(ε (sc)) forms
a unified tree structure rooted at atomic token c, where ε (sc) is the longest canonical suffix token
recognized by an Aho–Corasick automaton maintained over V .
Prefix Last-Token Condition and Monotonic Path Property. To determine whether node t ↑
SufSucTree(ε (sc)) represents the exact last token ω(sc), t must satisfy structural reachability and
priority dominance constraints.
Definition 1 (Prefix Last-Token Condition). For string s and suffix token t ↑ V , t satisfies the Prefix
Last-Token Condition if t is atomic, or if t is non-atomic and for prefix state k = ω(s ↔ suc(t)):

1. Reachability: k lies within the subtree of Fsuc rooted at pre(t) (including k = pre(t)).
2. Priority Dominance: If k ↗= pre(t), let u be the child of pre(t) on the ancestor path to k.
The canonical rule of t has strictly higher priority rank than u.

C ONCLUSION

We introduced Vectorized Direct-Interval Suffix Transducer (VD-STrans), an ultra-low-latency
incremental BPE framework. Exploiting the structural observation that > 99.1% of SuffixSuccessor Trees in real-world LLM vocabularies contain ↓ 8 nodes, VD-STrans flattens dynamic
tree topologies into 256-bit aligned Compact Flat Decision Vectors and resolves last-token selection
through a branchless AVX2 SIMD range kernel, an Atomic Fast-Path Bypass, and a CST fallback
for deep trees—eliminating pointer dereferences, branch mispredictions, and search-loop overheads.
Across 12 tokenizers and 3 text domains, VD-STrans attains a geometric-mean speedup of 1.123→
(up to 1.271→) over the state-of-the-art incremental algorithm and up to 23.64→ over heap baselines,
with 100% exact fidelity. Limitations and future work. VD-STrans is tuned for 256-bit AVX2, but
our K=4 topology analysis shows strong potential for 128-bit ARM Neon; porting the vectorized
primitives to ARM Neon and RISC-V Vector for edge deployment, and extending direct-interval
transduction to hardware-accelerated FPGA/GPU preprocessing, are promising directions.

Despite these strong empirical results, our approach has several limitations. First, our solver assumes
a static, upfront placement of models based on profiled request rates and Service Level Objectives
(SLOs). In highly dynamic production environments where workload patterns fluctuate unpredictably,
static placement may eventually lead to suboptimal resource utilization. Unlike systems that employ
dynamic execution-level chunking [Agrawal et al., 2023] or live migration of KV cache states [He
et al., 2025], our method does not currently adapt to real-time traffic shifts after the initial placement.
Second, our formulation assumes a uniform 80GB memory constraint across all GPUs. While this
reflects standard high-end accelerator deployments, it does not account for heterogeneous clusters
with mixed memory capacities or complex network topologies, which have been explored in recent
systems like Helix [Mei et al., 2024] and DisHelis [Zhang et al., 2026]. Finally, our model abstracts
GPU memory as contiguous pool, relying on the assumption that underlying systems handle
memory fragmentation effectively through techniques like virtual memory stitching or dynamic
paging [Prabhu et al., 2024].

Linearization vs. Iterative Reassignment We also explored alternative mathematical approaches
to handle the fractional KVPR objective. An Iterative Dinkelbach-style Reassignment approach
achieved a score of 26.1906, while a pure Fractional Optimization via Dinkelbach’s Iterative Linearization scored 26.0157. Both approaches underperformed compared to our target bisection method.
The fractional nature of the objective W/(M → S) ↑ K transforms perfectly into the linear constraint
W + K · S ↑ K · M . Bisection over the target K allows the solver to leverage robust, globally-aware
bin-packing heuristics rather than relying on iterative, gradient-like reassignments that are prone to
local optima in heavily constrained spaces.

Discussion and Conclusion

In this work, we addressed the critical challenge of minimizing Key-Value Cache Pressure (KVPR)
in multi-tenant Large Language Model (LLM) serving environments. By formulating the model
placement task as a strict non-linear fractional bin-packing problem, we introduced a deterministic,
three-phase algorithmic pipeline comprising greedy initialization, multi-heuristic linearized target
bisection, and local search refinement. Our approach effectively transforms the non-linear KVPR
objective into a solvable 1D capacity constraint. Experimental results demonstrate that our method
achieves a highly competitive KVPR score of 26.2560, outperforming sophisticated meta-evolutionary
frameworks such as OpenEvolve [Brown et al., 2025] and AdaEvolve [Cemri et al., 2026] while
maintaining a negligible computational overhead of approximately 0.021 seconds.

inflating execution time, proving to be a critical "finisher" for the algorithm.

In contrast, our proposed method is entirely deterministic and mathematically grounded in fractional
bin-packing principles [Alvim et al., 2004]. It achieves a 100% success rate in finding feasible placements with an exceptionally low execution time of approximately 0.021 seconds. By transforming
the non-linear KVPR objective into a linearized capacity constraint, our solver rapidly explores the
search space using standard 1D bin-packing heuristics [Fekete and Schepers, 2001], making it highly
suitable for dynamic, real-time cluster orchestration where millisecond-level scheduling latency is
required.

CoE Integrity Audit. The CoE Integrity Audit (Meng et al., 2026) is a post-hoc evaluation framework
that verifies whether claims in a generated paper are supported by its artifacts: code, empirical
outputs, and bibliography. It comprises four integrity checks: (1) Score verification, which evaluates
codebase reproducibility by comparing reported scores against those obtained from re-executing the
repository; (2) Specification compliance, which ensures that solution code adheres strictly to task rules
without reward hacking; (3) Reference verification, which guarantees that the bibliography contains
no hallucinated citations; and (4) Method-code alignment, which ensures that the paper faithfully
and accurately describes the codebase implementation. Following Meng et al. (2026), AI-generated
papers and their corresponding codebases must be rigorously verifiable across all four dimensions.
To guarantee these properties, we introduce three dedicated refinement agents alongside careful
agent prompt design. For score verification, the Coding Agent is prompted during the experimentation phase to output self-contained, reproducible scripts and execution instructions, ensuring full
reproducibility without requiring additional post-hoc refinement. For specification compliance, we
introduce a validation filter that uses the Coding Agent to detect and discard rule-violating solutions
immediately after experimentation. For reference verification, a search-augmented LLM identifies

Case Study. Prior retrieval augmented forecasting methods (Ning et al., 2025) attempt to improve
future predictions by fetching similar historical trajectories from external databases, but they treat
these retrieved curves as single, indivisible blocks. Such approach creates three critical bottlenecks: it
introduces unnatural jumps right at the boundary where current observations end and the forecast
begins, it tangles steady long-term trends with erratic short-term ripples, and it frequently degrades
performance when the retrieved data is noisy or irrelevant. To overcome these issues, ScientistTwo developed DynaSpec-RAG, a lightweight framework that refines forecasts dynamically. DynaSpec-RAG
resolves boundary jumps by smoothly anchoring retrieved curves to the final known observation,

uses real-FFT Fourier decomposition to split trajectories into clean macro-trends and seasonal details,
deploys a fine-grained gating network that evaluates how much to trust each frequency band at every
individual time step, and introduces a validation safety switch that dials retrieval influence down to
zero whenever it fails to add value (see Figure 11).
The novelty of DynaSpec-RAG lies in replacing crude copy-pasting with a surgical, frequency-aware
filter that actively anticipates and guards against bad data. Rather than assuming all retrieved history
is helpful, it independently regulates trust across different temporal scales and incorporates an explicit
do-no-harm safety fallback. This design provides high practical contribution: because it operates
strictly on the output space of frozen time-series foundation models, it requires no costly backbone
retraining and introduces only 0.27M trainable parameters. Despite this tiny footprint, it consistently
surpasses strong baselines across standard benchmarks (see Table 11) and transfers zero-shot to
completely unseen multi-domain datasets without retraining. For our evaluation of ScientistTwo,
this case demonstrates that the agent does not simply run shallow trial-and-error experiments; it
autonomously identifies root failure modes in existing literature and invests mathematically sound,
parameter-efficient solutions that earn acceptance from competitive peer review. In Appendices C
and D, we provide extensive qualitative artifacts, including generated ideas and limitations, evaluation
and ablation reports, Critic Agent feedback, CoE audit reports, and a complete generated paper.

5. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduce ScientistTwo, an autonomous multi-agent framework designed to advance
the frontier of scientific discovery without human intervention. By coupling holistic benchmark
evaluation and ablation-driven hypothesis refinement with a closed-loop peer-review and rebuttal
engine, ScientistTwo emulates the empirical rigor of expert human researchers. Across an extensive
evaluation on 107 competitive research challenges from top-tier venues (ICLR, ICML, NeurIPS),
ScientistTwo successfully advanced 80.4% of target problems, achieving an average relative improvement of 25.2% over human state-of-the-art baselines. Furthermore, the resulting manuscripts and
codebases consistently met top-tier conference acceptance thresholds while faithfully passing rigorous
multi-dimensional integrity audits. These results demonstrate that ScientistTwo moves beyond narrow
metric optimization to generate verifiable, publication-grade scientific contributions.
Limitations. While ScientistTwo consistently exceeds the acceptance threshold for standard conference publications (surpassing average scores from ICLR 2026 and NeurIPS 2025), it does not yet
consistently achieve the caliber of spotlight or oral presentations that introduce paradigm-shifting
conceptual breakthroughs. Future work will focus on expanding multi-agent exploration beyond local
algorithmic refinements toward discovering fundamentally new theoretical formulations.
It should also be noted that ScientistTwo costs approximately $3,800 to execute a single task.
This may be a limiting factor in the widespread use of ScientistTwo by academic laboratories or
independent researchers. On the other hand, improving the system’s cost-efficiency, such as by
replacing proprietary models with open-source models, is an interesting direction for future research.
